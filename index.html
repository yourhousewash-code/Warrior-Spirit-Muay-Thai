Why train Muay Thai

Stronger in the gym. Stronger outside it.

Muay Thai changes more than your fitness. People walk in to lose weight or learn to defend themselves, and stay for how it makes them feel.

Confidence

Walk taller

Standing your ground, taking a hard round and still going builds a quiet confidence that follows you to work, school and everywhere else. Knowing you can look after yourself changes how you carry yourself.

Fitness

Fight fit

Rounds on pads and bags work your heart, lungs, legs and core all at once. You build real strength, stamina and flexibility, burn serious calories, and never get bored doing it.

Team

A solo sport, a team gym

When the bell goes, it's you in there. But nobody gets there alone. Your training partners hold your pads, push you through the last round and are in your corner on fight night.

Head space

Leave the day at the door

You can't think about work while someone is throwing a kick at you. An hour on the mat clears your head, burns off stress and builds the discipline to keep showing up.

Two kinds of member, one team

Train for you. Or train to fight.

Some people join for fitness and a good crowd. Some want to get in the ring. We coach both, side by side, and each gets what they came for.

First visit

Nervous? Good.

Everyone here walked in for the first time once. These are the questions people ask most.

Do I need experience or fitness?

No. Come to Thursday's 7pm beginners class and work at your own pace. Fitness comes from training, not before it.

Will I have to spar?

Only when you want to and the coach says you're ready. Beginners work on pads and bags.

What do I bring?

Shorts, a T-shirt, water and a towel. We train barefoot. Hand wraps and gloves are worth getting early; ask a coach what to buy.

Is it OK for women?

Yes. Classes are mixed, friendly and closely coached, and Muay Thai is one of the most popular martial arts for women.

What age can kids start?

Kids Muay Thai is for ages 7 to 13, Monday to Thursday at 5:30pm, £65 a month.

Is personal training worth it?

If you want to progress faster, build confidence before joining a class, or prepare for a fight, yes. 1-to-1 sessions start from £40 an hour, with doubles (two people) from £45 and small groups from £55.

Our story

From Hoddesdon to Potters Bar

Warrior Spirit started life as Hoddesdon Muay Thai & K1 Kickboxing, Jay’s club on the top floor of Animal Gym in Hoddesdon. That’s where the team, the fighters and the way we train were built.

When the club moved to Potters Bar it took a new name, Warrior Spirit, and took over our friends’ gym, WMT Muay Thai, along the way. Same coaches, same fighters, same way of training. If you knew us in Hoddesdon you’ll see plenty of familiar faces, and plenty of old Hoddesdon shorts on fight night.

Also known as

  • Hoddesdon Muay Thai
  • Hoddesdon Muay Thai K1
  • WMT Muay Thai, Potters Bar

Now all Warrior Spirit Muay Thai & Kickboxing.

Owner & head coach

Jay Shepherd

18+ years teaching. 3rd Dan black belt.

James “Jay” Shepherd has been teaching kickboxing for over 18 years and holds a 3rd Dan black belt. He coaches people of every age, from seven-year-olds at their first class to adults getting fit and fighters getting ready for the ring.

He leads the fight team and runs fight camps, building each camp backwards from the fight date. His fighters have competed at amateur and semi-pro level, and he has taken kids to around a hundred interclubs, so he knows how to bring a nervous first-timer through their first bout as well as how to sharpen an experienced fighter.

In the gym he's the one holding the pads, watching your feet and telling you the one thing that will make the next round better. On fight night he's in the corner.

Training tips

Why we wrap every time

Wraps support the small bones in your hands and your wrist. Here's why they matter in every session, not just fight night.

Wrap firmly, but not so tight you lose feeling. Cover the wrist, the back of the hand and between the knuckles. Your coach will show you the method we use, so ask at your first class.

Fight news

In the corner at Nak Muay Rising

One minute between rounds. What the corner says, and why fighters train to listen.

Breathe, drink, one or two clear instructions, then back out. Those sixty seconds can turn a fight, and we practise them in camp so they're second nature on the night.

Nutritionby Steve

Five simple rules for fuelling training

No fad diets. Just the basics that keep you training hard and recovering well.

1. Eat a proper meal two to three hours before training. 2. Drink water through the day, not just in class. 3. Get some protein in every meal. 4. Plan food for busy days so you're not grabbing rubbish. 5. Sleep counts as much as food.

Interclubs to A-Class

How Muay Thai fighters progress in the UK, from a first friendly interclub to full-rules bouts. A plain-English guide for members and parents.

StartInterclubs

Friendly, controlled bouts between gyms, usually with protective kit. Great for kids and first-timers to experience the ring safely. We have taken kids to around a hundred.

Level 1N-Class

Novice bouts for fighters new to competing. Protective rules apply, so it is the usual first step onto a proper show. Dino and Leo make their N-Class debuts at Warriors of Hope 2.

Level 2C-Class

For fighters with a few bouts behind them. Rules are still protective but the level and intensity step up.

Level 3B-Class

Experienced fighters with fewer restrictions. Fights are harder and camps get longer.

TopA-Class

Full Muay Thai rules for the most experienced fighters, including semi-pro and pro level bouts.

Exact rules, round lengths and protective kit vary between promotions and governing bodies. Your coach will explain the rules for each fight.

How we move fighters up

Only when you’re ready

Nobody is pushed into fighting. Coaches move fighters up a level when their skills, fitness and experience say they’re ready, and every fight has a proper camp behind it.
